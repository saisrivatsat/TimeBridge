# Knowledge-base sources

TimeBridge Link's Learn tab is introductory, not exhaustive. Its historical and
technical statements are paraphrased from the institutions below.

## Measuring time

- [NIST: A Walk Through Time — Early Clocks](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/popular-links/walk-through-time/walk-through-time-early-clocks)
  documents early Egyptian obelisks, portable shadow clocks, water clocks, and
  the basic components shared by clocks.
- [NIST: A Revolution in Timekeeping](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/popular-links/walk-through-time/walk-through-time-revolution)
  describes the emergence of large European mechanical clocks and later
  accuracy improvements.
- [BIPM: Time resolutions](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure/annex-1/time)
  provides the official resolution history for the SI second and international
  time scales.

## Standard time and the prime meridian

- [Library of Congress: The Day of Two Noons](https://guides.loc.gov/this-month-in-business-history/november/day-of-two-noons)
  explains North American railroads' adoption of standard time on November 18,
  1883.
- [Royal Museums Greenwich: Why the Prime Meridian is in Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/time/what-prime-meridian-why-it-greenwich)
  explains the 1884 International Meridian Conference and the selection of the
  Greenwich meridian.
- [U.S. Department of Transportation: Uniform Time](https://www.transportation.gov/regulations/time-act)
  documents U.S. railway time, the Standard Time Act of 1918, federal time-zone
  oversight, and the Uniform Time Act of 1966.

## Modern civil time

- [BIPM: Time metrology](https://www.bipm.org/en/time-metrology)
  explains how UTC is produced from International Atomic Time and kept near
  Earth-rotation time.
- [NIST: Local Time FAQs](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/local-time-faqs)
  documents U.S. abbreviations, UTC offsets, daylight-saving dates, exceptions,
  and regulatory responsibilities.
- [IANA: Time zone and daylight-saving data](https://www.iana.org/time-zones/tz-link)
  describes the public time-zone database, location-based identifiers, and why
  political rule changes require ongoing updates.
